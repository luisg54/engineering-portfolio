Public-safe case study: Exact station identifiers, camera counts, cable lengths, internal layouts, coverage maps, data systems, and production imagery are intentionally omitted.

Problem

Scaling constraint

A handheld, multi-person data-collection process could support early testing but was not practical to scale across production. The hardware had to move to repeatable station-mounted systems without obstructing operators, safety devices, maintenance access, or production flow.

My role

Alongside another intern, I translated evolving field requirements into modular hardware and co-led the physical deployment. My ownership spanned mechanical CAD, attachment strategy, compute and battery-pack mounting, station surveys, assembly, wiring, commissioning, troubleshooting, iteration, and handoff.

Mechanical architecture

Sanitized system illustration; not an actual factory layout or camera-coverage map.

Adjustable camera mounts

Three rigid architectures with lockable multi-axis adjustment for repeatable aiming and field service.

Swappable station interfaces

Interchangeable bases for common fixed structures, allowing one core mount design to adapt across varied stations.

Compute and battery-pack hardware

Tool-free, 3D-printed mounts with locating features for fast installation, service, and replacement.

Deployment and iteration

  • Surveyed real station geometry and selected safe attachment locations around live production constraints.
  • Released designs to a print farm and standardized fasteners, adapters, and installation hardware.
  • Co-led assembly and wiring, then commissioned systems during limited production-access windows.
  • Iterated camera angles and mounting geometry from early data-quality feedback and field issues.
  • Scaled the hardware to 20 mounted stations and documented a repeatable deployment package for the next engineer.

What made the system scalable

Modularity

Shared core hardware with swappable interfaces reduced redesign effort across different station structures.

Field maintainability

Tool-free pack mounts, standardized hardware, and documented installation steps reduced dependence on tribal knowledge.

Lessons

Prototype success is not deployment success.Temporary mounts proved feasibility, but scaling required rigid locking, repeatable interfaces, serviceability, and controlled installation.
Field feedback belongs inside the design loop.Camera-angle and access feedback changed the hardware after deployment; the system improved because validation continued after installation.
A handoff is an engineering deliverable.The next engineer needs selection logic, assembly steps, validation checks, and known failure modes—not just CAD files.
